Pasture, Rangeland, Forage — rainfall index
About these views

One map, five views — pick one from the Show dropdown inside the map:

  • County multiplier — CBV ($/acre) — the RMA per-acre county dollar figure that scales PRF protection (acres × productivity factor × coverage level × CBV).
  • Best win rate (%) — share of historical years the best interval allocation returned a positive net.
  • Best return per $1 of protection — average net return per $1, the sweep's stored, scale-free figure.
  • Best return per ACRE ($) — computed as best net × CBV × coverage level × productivity factor; the productivity factor is your own election (RMA allows 60–150%, default 100%) and appears as an input on the page.
  • Commission per acre ($) — your side of the deal, not the producer's: CBV × coverage × productivity × Σ(allocation × premium rate) × commission %, priced on the optimizer's recommended (best-net) allocation. Agent commission is a percent of total premium, and premium is the CBV scaled by a premium rate the CBV view leaves out — one that varies several-fold between grids at the same coverage, so this map ranks counties differently from the CBV map. Pick which AIP's rate to apply with the AIP selector; rates are your own negotiated numbers — use Commission rates… to edit them.

Optimizer figures come from simulating all 59,536 valid interval-allocation policies (2–5 non-adjacent two-month intervals, 5% steps, 10–60% each, summing to 100%) per grid over the RMA rainfall-index history; a county shows the best result among the grids it touches. One intended-use control (Grazing / Haying / Haying-Irrigated) drives both datasets. Counties with no data for the current selection stay neutral — nothing is inferred.

Interval analyzer — per-interval heat map and recommendation

About this view

The county map ranks counties on the best allocation it found. This is the layer underneath: every two-month interval scored on its own for one grid, so you can see which intervals are actually carrying the result. Pick the metric — win rate, return per $1 of protection, or return per acre — and the heat map, the year-by-year matrix and the recommendation below all follow it. Window ends picks the last year of the lookback and Lookback picks 5, 10, 15 or 20 years back from it; everything is re-scored over exactly those years and the allocation search is re-run, which is the question a fixed illustration can't answer: does this interval pay because of the whole record, or because of one wet decade — and would it have said the same thing if you had asked five years ago? Save client deck turns the grid, county and use above, with the coverage, productivity and window set in the analyzer, into a three-slide PowerPoint or PDF: a title slide, the five most consistent and five best-return allocations, and a closing slide.

All allocations — every interval split for one grid, sorted

About this view

The interval analyzer shows the one allocation that wins. This is the whole search behind it: every admissible allocation for the grid, county and use chosen here (2–5 non-adjacent intervals, 5% steps, 10% up to the county cap on each, and Jan–Feb no more than 25% from crop year 2027), with its average net return per acre, win rate and return per $1 over the window you pick. Sort by orders them by any of the three metrics, ties broken by the other; rank #1 is the analyzer's recommendation. Save as CSV writes the rows in the optimizer workbook's columns (grid_county_use, combinations, proportions, average_net_return, win rate, state).

Herd size requirements — FSA acres per animal unit

About this view

How much of the insured ground a given herd size needs, by county: FSA's National Crop Table publishes Acres Per Animal Unit — the acres of a given crop/type needed to carry one animal unit for the grazing period — for every crop/type it prices for grazing under NAP/ARC-PLC. This is not an RMA table and not scoped to PRF ground specifically (neither agency publishes a carrying-capacity figure for PRF acreage as such); GRASS — Native is offered as the default because it is the closest available analog to native rangeland and has the widest county coverage. Where a county carries more than one FSA planting period for the same crop/type, this map shows the largest (most acres required) — the conservative figure for a herd grazed there continuously through its leanest period — and the tooltip gives the full min–max spread and how many periods it came from. Non-irrigated only: PRF's own Grazing intended use has no irrigated/non-irrigated split either.
